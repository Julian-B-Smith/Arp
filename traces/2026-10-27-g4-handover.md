# g4-handover — the edge fuzz's G4 "breach" was the test host's; G4 is strict again

- **Queue item:** T-007 (M3), the finding recorded as "FOUND 2026-10-27, not yet
  diagnosed (candidate G4 breach)": edge fuzz session 20 (PR #29, `m3/randomness`).
- **Verdict for session 20:** (c), a test-host artifact. Not (a), a core bug, and
  not (b), a spec gap. `core/`, `lab/` and `spec/` are untouched.
- **But G4 is not proven:** the critic round found a separate, real G4 breach on
  musically identical input. It is a spec gap, (b), now proposed erratum **E13**
  in `docs/spec-errata.md`, pending the human. See "Critic round" below.
- **Why:** `SimHost::rescaled` (tests/core/harness.h, before this change) replayed
  the host script's ops at the ROUNDED scaled sample. So the position a stop holds
  was `posAt(rounded sample)`, and at 44.1 kHz that is a different musical
  position. 8 kHz positions are whole samples at 48/96/192 kHz (×6/×12/×24), but
  not at 44.1 kHz (×441/80).
  In session 20 the ops were applied out of time order: stop at 17500 (then
  start at 20500), then a stop at 12500. So the stopped segment from 17500 holds
  P(17500), the playing extrapolation:
  - at 48 kHz: sample 105000, tick **67200** exactly. That is 35 × 1920 (rate 1/32).
  - at 44.1 kHz: 17500 → 96468.75 → sample **96469**, tick **67200.174**
    (= 49392128/735).

  At the restart (20500) the core re-anchors to the first grid step at or after
  the position: `lastEvaluated_ = kAtOrAfter(...) - 1` at core/src/core.cpp:470
  and :620, with `kAtOrAfter` at core/include/arp/core.h:267. That is exactly
  SPEC §4.5 ("the first grid step at or after"). So it picks step 35 at 48 kHz and
  step 36 at 44.1 kHz. The 48 kHz note at tick 68125 is step 35 swung
  (67200 + 925). The 44.1 kHz note at 69120 is step 36.

  The core was given two different inputs and answered both correctly. The same
  thing happens with randomness off because it has nothing to do with D-036.
  Rounding direction is why only session 20 showed it: a stop rounded DOWN (e.g.
  12500 → 68906, tick 47999.83) still picks the same step "at or after".
- **Fix (tests only):**
  - `SimHost::rescaled` now builds the new host from the source's segments. A
    position the script holds or sets (a stop, the resume after it, a seek) is
    the source's exact posNum × newSr / sr. That conversion is exact, and an abort
    guards it. A tempo change while playing (a playing segment whose base is its
    predecessor's continuation) stays continuous at the new rate, as a real host
    is.
  - The old replay is kept as `bySample` for the control.
  - `testEdgeFuzz`: the `g4Core` tolerance and its randomness-off twin are
    removed. G4 is strict: 27 of 27 sync-mode sessions are identical at
    44.1/48/96/192 kHz.
  - New `testHandOver` (test_random.cpp) is a minimal known answer: 120 bpm, two
    keys held, stop at 17500 and start at 20500 (8 kHz). The first note after the
    restart is step 35 (tick 67200) at all four rates, and the tick streams are
    identical.
  - The must-fail control is the by-sample host at 44.1 kHz. It restarts on
    step 36 (tick 69120), and G4 reads red. Verified: the plant fires.
- **Digest:** `tests/core/stream_digest_random.txt` moved (760 → 766 runs). It was
  regenerated from the digest's own origin core with
  `bash tools/core_digest.sh regen-random ed943b2`, NOT from the working tree, so
  the file still pins the pre-optimisation core's output, now on the corrected
  inputs. What changed, by sequence alignment against 434ae06:
  - 11 edge-fuzz runs at 44.1 kHz. Their held positions were sub-tick off before.
  - Session 20's two twin runs are gone, and its 96/192 kHz runs are now made
    (the strict check no longer stops at 44.1).
  - 6 appended `testHandOver` runs.

  Every 8/48/96/192 kHz run is byte-identical. The main stream digest (42226 runs,
  including 44.1 kHz replays of the M1b sessions) and the bench digests are
  unchanged. This is not a behaviour change of the core; it is flagged for the

- **Alternatives rejected:**
  - Moving the fuzz's op positions to samples that are exact at every rate
    (multiples of 80 at 8 kHz). It hides the class, not the cause, and it changes
    every rate's sessions.
  - Converting every segment exactly, tempo changes included. It would add
    sub-tick re-bases at 44.1 kHz to sessions like mixSession, which a real host
    does not produce.
  - Sorting the ops by time. It changes the musical script at every rate. The
    out-of-order "stopped jump" is legitimate host input (a locate while stopped).
- **Critic round (Opus, read-only, independent probes): APPROVE-WITH-NOTES.**
  - It confirmed the session-20 diagnosis: 6 free-clock notes during the stop at
    every rate, and 0 divergences over 16,560 stop/start × tempo × gate × swing
    sweeps with the exact host.
  - It confirmed the digest spot-check: 11 lines at 44.1 kHz changed, and no line
    at 8, 48, 96 or 192 kHz changed.
  - **H1 → E13 (verified here too, with the critic's reproducer).** A key released
    0.70 tick after step 11 (209 bpm, 1/32, 8 kHz sample 3158, which is exact at
    48/96/192 kHz). Step 11 plays key 60 at 48 kHz and key 67 at 96 and 192 kHz.
    At 48 kHz the step's rounded-up sample is the release's sample, and the core
    takes input before the grid step on a tie (core.cpp:696). The spec orders
    output events at one offset (§1.1), but never input against a step. So this
    is (b): the spec is not edited, and the options are written up in E13. Of the
    critic's 3000 random sessions, 15–17 diverge this way. The fuzz's 40 seeds
    happen to miss it, and the fuzz comment now says so.
  - **M1 (follow-up, not changed):** above about 172 bpm at 44.1 kHz, two offs
    whose ticks are one apart can share a sample. §1.1 then orders them by
    ascending id, and the order-sensitive `tickStreamOf` reads that as red even
    though every tick is equal. The 40 seeds don't reach it. Fix in the
    comparator: sort within a same-sample group before comparing.
  - **M2 (partly done):** the `srTickMath` plant now compares 44.1, 96 and 192 kHz
    each against 48. But its skew is `sr / 44100`, which is 1 at both 44.1 and
    48 kHz, so no existing plant can show a 44.1 kHz core fault. A new plant needs
    a `core.cpp` edit (copy-in-hashed): follow-up.
